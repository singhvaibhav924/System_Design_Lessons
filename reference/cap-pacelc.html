CAP and PACELC

The short version of Lesson 0004.

CAP’s scope

During a network partition, a system cannot guarantee both linearizability and a successful response to every request at every non-failing node.

DDIA Ch. 9, PDF pp. 358–360

C · Consistency

In CAP, read/write behavior is linearizable: each operation appears to take effect once, in a shared order.

A · Availability

Every request sent to a non-failing node eventually returns a non-error response. This is not an uptime percentage.

P · Partition

A network fault stops groups of machines from communicating. It is not data partitioning/sharding.

Favor consistency

During a partition, some reads or writes wait or fail rather than risk violating linearizability.

Favor availability

During a partition, reachable replicas continue answering; responses can be stale or writes may need reconciliation.

“Pick two”

Misleading. Partitions are not optional. The practical choice is what to do with requests while partitioned.

PACELC

If Partition: Availability vs Consistency. Else: Latency vs Consistency. It adds the normal-operation coordination cost to CAP.

Daniel Abadi, “Consistency Tradeoffs…”

Ask per operation

During a partition, should this read/write wait or fail, or can it use possibly stale/conflicting data? In normal operation, should it wait for stronger freshness or answer faster from a nearby replica?
