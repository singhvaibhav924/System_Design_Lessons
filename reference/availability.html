Availability cheat sheet

The short version of Lesson 0003.

Availability

Can I use it now? Fraction of time up, or (better for big systems) fraction of requests that succeed ("yield").

Reliability

Keeps working correctly (right answers, good enough speed, secure) despite faults. Available ≠ reliable: fast wrong answers are still a failure.

DDIA p. 6

Durability

Stored data isn't lost. S3: 11 nines durable, 4 nines available. A cache: available, not durable.

Fault vs failure

Fault = one component misbehaves. Failure = the system stops serving users. Goal: stop faults becoming failures.

Correlated faults

Bugs, bad configs, leap seconds, cascades: they hit every copy at once, so redundancy doesn't help. Gradual rollouts and fast rollback do.

Combining parts

Series: multiply (0.9994 ≈ 99.6%). Parallel: 1 − (1 − a)k (two 99% copies → 99.99%). This is a best case that assumes independent failures and working failover.

MTBF / MTTR

Availability = MTBF / (MTBF + MTTR). Faster recovery counts as much as failing less often.

SLI · SLO · SLA

SLI = the measure. SLO = the target for it. SLA = a contract with consequences. Keep the internal SLO stricter than the SLA.

Google SRE book Ch. 4

Error budget

1 − SLO. Budget left → ship. Budget spent → releases halted, work on reliability. 100% is never the target.

Google SRE book Ch. 3

Graceful degradation

Lose features, not the whole product. ML version, "smooth failing": a fallback model, heuristic or cached predictions after X ms.
